About me

I learn by making things, then asking why they worked.

I studied Computer Science at Nanjing Tech University. Most of what I remember best came from building projects, debugging them, and trying again when the first version was messy.

The short version
I’m more interested in understanding than looking impressive.

I like software because there is always something concrete to test. An idea either becomes useful, breaks somewhere, or teaches me what I missed.

That is also how I approach learning. I prefer building a real version early, seeing what feels wrong, and improving it instead of waiting for the perfect plan.

Outside code, I care a lot about people, faith, travel, and the small experiences that change how I think.

How I usually work

Simple rules I keep coming back to.

01

Start with the real problem

Before adding features, I try to understand what someone actually needs to do.

02

Build a real version

A working version gives better feedback than a long list of assumptions.

03

Keep it understandable

I would rather have code and interfaces I can explain clearly than complexity for its own sake.

04

Stay open to changing my mind

If testing shows the first idea was wrong, that is useful information, not a failure.
